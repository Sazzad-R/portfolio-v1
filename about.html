BACKGROUND 01 / 03

About my work

I am a master's student in Geographical Information Science at the University at Buffalo, with previous master's and bachelor's degrees in Geography and Environment from Shahjalal University of Science and Technology in Bangladesh.

My early research included household field interviews on fishing livelihoods and an undergraduate thesis on small-scale tea cultivation and adoption decisions. Later, as a Field Research Officer with the Center for Natural Resource Studies, I evaluated development and livelihood programming and encountered the importance of institutional conditions in shaping outcomes.

At Buffalo, my work has expanded into neighborhood change, GIS-based planning, remote sensing, spatial statistics, and machine learning. I am increasingly interested in moving from identifying geographic patterns to explaining the social, economic, and institutional mechanisms that produce them.
